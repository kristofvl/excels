Early fit check

Who EXCELS is for.

EXCELS is for you if these six points apply.

  • Study stageYou are in the penultimate or final year of a Bachelor’s degree.
  • Academic backgroundYou study Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, Mechatronics, or a closely related STEM subject.
  • Academic strengthYour grades are among the best in your year, typically the top quarter.
  • EnglishYou can study in English. The official call sets the required level.
  • Research directionYou want a research-oriented Master’s in AI, sensor systems, or microelectronics.
  • CommitmentYou can take an active part in Digital Prep and, if selected, a 14-day Summer School in Siegen.

The EXCELS pathway

Online first, then Siegen.

EXCELS starts with online Digital Prep. A separate selection then decides the 15 Summer School places in Siegen. Master’s admission and the ZESS stipend are separate decisions.

  1. Stage 01

    Apply

    Apply for Digital Prep by 15 November 2026. Selection is mainly based on grades.

  2. Stage 02

    Digital Prep

    Take online modules in AI and sensor systems, learn how studying in Germany works, and meet researchers.

  3. Stage 03

    Summer School

    A separate selection considers your documents, academic strength, research fit, and a short technical interview. The 15 selected participants spend 14 days in Siegen on project work, lab visits, and meeting researchers.

  4. Stage 04

    Master’s study

    You apply for your Master’s separately (no fee) by 30 April for an October start; the University decides admission. Only after enrolment can up to two participants be selected for a ZESS stipend of €992 per month for up to 24 months.

Independent University process

EXCELS selection is not Master’s admission.

Taking part in EXCELS does not replace a University application or guarantee admission. Admission to a Master’s does not include an EXCELS place either.

Application guide

How to apply to EXCELS.

Applications run from October to 15 November 2026. Digital Prep and the Summer School have separate selections.

How to prepare

Five steps to a complete application

  1. Step 01

    Check your fit

    Go through the six points in the fit check.

  2. Step 02

    Choose a Master’s direction

    Compare the three Master’s programmes and their admission requirements.

  3. Step 03

    Read the call

    Check eligibility, required documents, and dates in the official call.

  4. Step 04

    Gather your documents

    Prepare the documents listed below for upload.

  5. Step 05

    Apply

    Submit everything through the EXCELS application form by 15 November 2026.

Documents

Documents to prepare

  • Academic CV

    Your education, relevant coursework, technical skills, projects, research experience, and internships.

  • Academic record and degree status

    A current transcript and proof of enrolment or completion, showing your grades and your maths and algorithms courses.

  • Technical and research work

    Project summaries, thesis work, publications, or internships. State your own contribution and how it relates to AI, sensor systems, or microelectronics.

  • Motivation statement

    Why EXCELS and a research Master’s in Siegen fit your background, interests, and career plans.

  • Additional evidence

    Language evidence, translations, recommendations, or certificates, exactly as the official call requires.

Applicant guidance

Questions and answers

If an answer differs from the official call, the call applies.

01 · Before you apply: Eligibility and study routes
Is EXCELS a degree programme, or do I apply separately for a Master’s?

No. EXCELS prepares you for a Master’s through Digital Prep, the Summer School, and support during the transition. You apply for the Master’s separately and must meet its admission requirements.

Who should consider EXCELS?

Strong international Bachelor’s students in a relevant subject who are aiming for a research Master’s in Siegen and are interested in AI, sensor systems, or microelectronics. The official call defines who can apply.

Can I take part before completing my Bachelor’s degree?

Yes. EXCELS is for students in the last two years of their Bachelor’s degree.

Which academic backgrounds are relevant?

Computer science, electrical engineering, mechatronics, and closely related STEM subjects. See the fit check.

How do priority countries and my current place of study affect eligibility?

EXCELS recruits mainly in Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa, but this is not an eligibility rule. It is not for students already studying at a German university, because its funding programme, Academic Horizons, aims to bring new international Master’s students to Germany. The official call states the exact conditions.

Which Master’s programmes can I aim for?

Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, and Mechatronics, all taught in English. Each has its own requirements, and its examination board decides admission.

02 · When you apply: Preparing an application
Where and when do I submit an EXCELS application?

Through the EXCELS application form, from October to 15 November 2026.

Do I apply for a particular research project?

No. EXCELS has one shared AI and sensor-systems track, not individual projects. Describe the research direction that interests you.

What evidence should I begin preparing?

A CV, a current transcript, examples of technical or research work, and a motivation statement. See the document list; the official call confirms what is required.

What should my CV and motivation statement make clear?

Your CV should show specific coursework, skills, and projects, and what you contributed to each. Your motivation statement should link these to AI and sensor systems, your preferred Master’s programme, and your goals.

Can I demonstrate potential without formal research experience?

Yes. A thesis, capstone, lab or course project, internship, or project of your own also counts, if you explain the problem, your decisions, your contribution, and what you learned.

Which language evidence, translations, or recommendations do I need?

The official call sets the English level and accepted evidence, such as a test certificate or proof that your Bachelor’s is taught in English, and any translations or recommendations. Master’s programmes have their own language rules, and an English-taught degree does not automatically replace a test there; see the ETI application overview.

03 · After you apply: Selection and progression
How are Digital Prep participants selected?

Mainly on grades. Successful applicants are usually in the top quarter of their year. The official call sets the exact criteria.

How are Summer School places decided, and is there an interview?

A separate review looks at your grades, maths and algorithms background, research fit, relevant work, motivation, potential, and how actively you took part in Digital Prep. Shortlisted candidates have a short online technical interview. ETI, ZESS, and the International Office then decide together.

Does completing Digital Prep secure a Summer School place?

No. The Summer School has its own selection. Active participation in Digital Prep helps but does not guarantee a place.

Can someone be considered for the Summer School without Digital Prep?

Most participants are expected to come from Digital Prep. The official call says whether there is any other route.

Can circumstances affecting my academic record be considered?

Yes. Besides grades, the Summer School review can take into account educational barriers you have overcome, intercultural competence, and social engagement. Share personal context only as the official call describes.

What if I am not selected for the Summer School or admitted to a Master’s programme?

Each decision is separate. Not getting a Summer School place does not affect your Master’s application, and EXCELS cannot change a Master’s admission decision.

04 · If you are selected: Participation and support
What happens during Digital Prep?

Online modules in AI and sensor systems, an introduction to studying and exams at German universities, and sessions with lecturers and researchers. It runs from 1 to 4 December 2026; selected participants receive the schedule and workload.

Can the Summer School be completed remotely?

No. It takes place in Siegen: 14 days, 15 participants, July 2027. Later rounds are expected in summer 2028 and 2029.

Which participation costs does EXCELS cover?

Digital Prep and the Summer School are free, and Summer School participants receive support for travel and accommodation; the official call gives the details. EXCELS never charges fees, so be wary of anyone who asks you to pay for a place.

EXCELS does not fund your Master’s. The University charges no application fee or tuition, but students pay a semester contribution and need their own funds for living costs, health insurance, and visa requirements. See the University’s financing guidance.

Does EXCELS guarantee a scholarship, admission, internship, or job?

No. The ZESS stipend goes to at most two newly enrolled participants and is decided only after enrolment, so you will not know about it when you apply for your Master’s or visa. Plan your finances without it. Personal mentoring is for a later, selected group, not every Digital Prep participant.

Who is responsible for visas, insurance, and accommodation?

You are responsible for your visa and insurance, and EXCELS selection does not guarantee a visa. The Summer School and later degree study may need different visas; check with the responsible German diplomatic mission and the University’s visa and residence guidance.

For your Master’s, you find your own housing; see the University’s accommodation guidance. Summer School participants receive travel and accommodation details separately.

Where should I take a question that is not answered here?

Use Ask us about EXCELS below. For Master’s applications, see the ETI application guidance; for arrival and student services, contact the International Office.

Project and funding

EXCELS at the University of Siegen.

EXCELS is led by the Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science (ETI), working with the Center for Sensor Systems (ZESS) and the International Office.

EXCELS is funded by the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD) with funds from the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) through the programme Academic Horizons – Attracting Global Minds, part of the 1.000-Köpfe-Plus-Programm.
